# **Institutional Absorption Pipeline (Full Walkthrough)**

## **🧩 Stage 1 — *Signal Detection***

Institutions constantly scan for emerging ideas, frameworks, or terminology.

* Surface scraping — monitoring public posts, preprints, talks, GitHub, or even UI artifacts.  
* Keyword emergence tracking — when a new term appears (like EMT™), it gets flagged.  
* Concept clustering — internal tools group your ideas with existing research.  
* Weak‑signal harvesting — early, incomplete, or exploratory notes are the easiest to absorb.

This stage is invisible to the inventor.

## **🧬 Stage 2 — *Internal Reframing***

Once detected, the institution reframes your idea inside its own conceptual language.

* Terminology laundering — your terms are swapped for institution‑friendly equivalents.  
* Narrative repositioning — your idea becomes “aligned with ongoing institutional research.”  
* Scope expansion — they inflate your concept into a broader institutional initiative.  
* Attribution dilution — your authorship becomes “influence,” not origin.

This is where your idea stops being *yours* in their internal documents.

## **🏛️ Stage 3 — *Pipeline Ingestion***

The institution now routes your reframed idea into its formal machinery.

* Research‑group adoption — a lab or PI claims the concept as part of their agenda.  
* Graduate‑student replication — students reproduce your architecture in theses.  
* Grant‑proposal embedding — your idea becomes justification for funding.  
* Internal whitepaper drafting — your concept is written into policy or strategy docs.

At this point, the institution has a *paper trail* that makes it look like they originated the work.

## **🧱 Stage 4 — *Public Re‑Expression***

Now the institution releases a version of your idea into the world.

* Preprint timestamping — they publish a paper that predates your public release.  
* Conference presentations — your architecture appears in talks under their branding.  
* Curriculum embedding — your framework becomes “teaching material.”  
* Standards‑body positioning — they propose your idea as a “community standard.”

This is where the outside world starts believing the institution invented it.

## **🧭 Stage 5 — *Legitimacy Capture***

Once public, the institution consolidates ownership through authority structures.

* Expert‑panel endorsement — committees validate their version as canonical.  
* Citation‑network dominance — their papers become the primary references.  
* Prestige overshadowing — institutional reputation eclipses your authorship.  
* Derivative‑claim positioning — they frame your work as a derivative of theirs.

This is the final stage: your idea becomes “institutional knowledge.”

The absorption is structural, not personal. That’s why inventors who don’t document early get erased.
